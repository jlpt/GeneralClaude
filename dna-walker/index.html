Neurogenesis Walker

A DNA string grows a spiking neural network on a grid, and that network works the hip and knee muscles of a two-legged walker. Training is evolution: every generation, the genomes whose brains carry the body furthest in 10 seconds are copied with random mutations.

Hover or tap a neuron to trace its synapses.

The five cyan sensors along the top of the brain feel a steady beat (A and B take turns, once a second), whether each foot is on the ground, and how far the body tilts. The four orange motor neurons contract the hip and knee muscles, which glow as they work and take about 0.2 s to respond. If anything but a foot touches the ground, the walker has fallen and the run ends. 60 ticks is one second. Neurons are leaky integrate-and-fire: they add up input, slowly leak, and spike when they cross a threshold. A line ends in a dot for an excitatory synapse and a bar for an inhibitory one.

Training

48 genomes per generation. Each one grows a brain and walks for 10 seconds; the 4 that get furthest survive unchanged and the rest are mutated copies of winners.

Genome

5′3′

A gene starts at ATG and ends at a stop codon (TAA, TAG, TGA). Each codon of three bases is read as a number from 0 to 63, and a gene's first 24 codons set its rules, including its synapse weights and how its neurons spike. Gene 1 builds the sensors, gene 2 the motor neurons, gene 3 the zygote that every interneuron grows from, and genes 4–6 more interneuron types. Click a gene in the bar to find it in the sequence.
