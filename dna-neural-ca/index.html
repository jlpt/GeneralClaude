Neurogenesis Automaton

A DNA string grows a spiking neural network on a grid, and that network steers a swimmer toward food. Training is evolution: every generation, the genomes whose brains collect the most food are copied with random mutations.

Hover or tap a neuron to trace its synapses.

The five cyan sensors along the top of the brain smell food at −60°, −30°, 0°, 30° and 60° from the swimmer's heading. The two orange motor neurons turn it left or right; it always swims forward. Neurons are leaky integrate-and-fire: they add up input, slowly leak, and spike when they cross a threshold. A line ends in a dot for an excitatory synapse and a bar for an inhibitory one.

Genome

5′3′

A gene starts at ATG and ends at a stop codon (TAA, TAG, TGA). Each codon of three bases is read as a number from 0 to 63, and a gene's first 24 codons set its rules, including its synapse weights and how its neurons spike. Gene 1 builds the sensors, gene 2 the motor neurons, gene 3 the zygote that every interneuron grows from, and genes 4–6 more interneuron types. Click a gene in the bar to find it in the sequence.
