Neurogenesis Ball Toss

One cell and a random growth rule grow a jointed body with a nervous system inside it. One ball is dropped onto its back and four more lie on the ground nearby. The score adds up each ball's longest time in the air, so juggling one ball doesn't add up: to score more, the creature has to get to the others and get them up, with no hands. Reaching a ball on the ground earns a little too. Evolution keeps the growth rules whose creatures score most, and the genome bench below lets you change the numbers yourself and grow the result.

All the numbers

Growth

Over 40 steps, every cell reads its neighbours and updates its 18 numbers with the same small network, starting from a single cell on a 30 × 42 grid. Every 3 × 3 patch with enough living cells becomes a joint, joined by a bone to the patch it grew from.

A joint becomes bone, muscle or eye, whichever of those numbers its cells hold most of. Muscle joints are hinges; eyes are rigid and see.

Senses and nerves

Any small cell may grow a nerve cell. They form a spiking recurrent network: each has a charge that leaks, fires at a threshold, and sends weighted spikes only to nerve cells touching it. A muscle joint bends when the nerve cells in its patch fire.

Here nerve cells can feel touch (in joints on the ground or touching a ball), a slow pacemaker rhythm, and sight (in an eye, brighter the more balls ahead of it and the nearer they are). There is nothing to eat, so hunger stays at zero.

The balls and evolution

Each ball is half a joint's weight and 10 cm across. One is dropped onto the creature's back, because no random creature ever lifts a ball off the ground and evolution needs a first step. The other four lie 0.14 to 1.44 m beyond either end, and reaching one earns 0.1: without that, evolution only ever threw the ball on the back. Balls pass through each other, can leave a limb at most twice as fast as the limb moves, and never go faster than 8 m/s.

Each generation adds small random changes to the genome, tests every copy in parallel workers, and moves toward the changes that threw farthest. When a copy beats the best so far on the re-test, the search jumps to it.

Take a genome to the Python version

Press Copy as text for Python, save it as best.txt in the project folder, and turn it into a file render.py can draw:

python -c "import base64, numpy as np; np.save('best.npy', np.frombuffer(base64.b64decode(open('best.txt').read()), np.float32))"
python render.py best.npy --out best.gif --task balls --world 1000
