Neurogenesis Thrower

One cell and a random growth rule grow a jointed body with a nervous system inside it. A ball is dropped onto its back, and the creature has no hands: to score, it has to fling the ball off with its body. The score is how far the ball flies through the air. Evolution keeps the growth rules whose creatures throw farthest, and the genome bench below lets you change the numbers yourself and grow the result.

All the numbers

Growth

Over 40 steps, every cell reads its neighbours and updates its 18 numbers with the same small network, starting from a single cell on a 30 × 42 grid. Every 3 × 3 patch with enough living cells becomes a joint, joined by a bone to the patch it grew from.

A joint becomes bone, muscle or eye, whichever of those numbers its cells hold most of. Muscle joints are hinges; eyes are rigid and see.

Senses and nerves

Any small cell may grow a nerve cell. They form a spiking recurrent network: each has a charge that leaks, fires at a threshold, and sends weighted spikes only to nerve cells touching it. A muscle joint bends when the nerve cells in its patch fire.

Here nerve cells can feel touch (in joints on the ground or touching the ball), a slow pacemaker rhythm, and sight (in an eye, brighter the nearer the ball ahead of it). There is nothing to eat, so hunger stays at zero.

The ball and evolution

The ball is half a joint's weight and 10 cm across. After the creature settles, it's dropped onto the top of the body and settles too. It can leave a limb at most twice as fast as the limb moves, and never faster than 8 m/s. With the ball starting on the ground, evolution only ever learned to kick it along, so for now it starts on the creature's back.

Each generation adds small random changes to the genome, tests every copy in parallel workers, and moves toward the changes that threw farthest. When a copy beats the best so far on the re-test, the search jumps to it.

Take a genome to the Python version

Press Copy as text for Python, save it as best.txt in the project folder, and turn it into a file render.py can draw:

python -c "import base64, numpy as np; np.save('best.npy', np.frombuffer(base64.b64decode(open('best.txt').read()), np.float32))"
python render.py best.npy --out best.gif --task throw --world 1000
