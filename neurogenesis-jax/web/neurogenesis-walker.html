Growth

The genome is about 2,100 numbers: the weights of one tiny neural network that every cell runs. Over 32 steps, each cell reads its neighbours and updates its 16 numbers, starting from a single cell.

Each living cell becomes a joint, joined by a bone to the neighbour it grew from. Bone cells are rigid; muscle cells are hinges, and their numbers say which way they bend.

Nerves

A cell may also grow a nerve cell. Only nerve cells are neurons, and a spike can only pass to a nerve cell touching it, one cell per tick, so long nerves have a delay.

A muscle joint bends when the nerve cell inside it fires. Nerves in the feet feel the ground, and every nerve cell can feel a slow pacemaker rhythm. The cluster in the middle is the brain only because that is where the nerves meet.

Evolution

Each generation adds small random changes to the genome, grows and tests every copy in parallel workers, and moves the genome toward the changes that walked farthest (an evolution strategy).

The starter was trained to grow a long body with a hip and knee in each leg and a nerve to each joint. From there, evolution may reshape the body, rewire the nerves or cut them.

Take the best walker to the Python version

Copy its genome, save it as best.txt in the project folder, and turn it into a file render.py can draw:

python -c "import base64, numpy as np; np.save('best.npy', np.frombuffer(base64.b64decode(open('best.txt').read()), np.float32))"
python render.py best.npy --out best.gif
