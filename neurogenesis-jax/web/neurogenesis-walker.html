Neurogenesis Walker

Everything starts from one cell and a random growth rule. The cell grows into a jointed body with a nervous system of tiny nerve cells inside it, and a joint only moves when a nerve reaches it and fires. Nothing is designed in advance: evolution keeps the growth rules whose creatures walk farthest.

Growth

The genome is about 2,100 numbers: the weights of one tiny neural network that every cell runs. Over 40 steps, each cell reads its neighbours and updates its 16 numbers, starting from a single cell on a 30 × 42 grid.

Every 3 × 3 patch with enough living cells becomes a joint, joined by a bone to the patch it grew from. Bone joints are rigid; muscle joints are hinges, and their numbers say which way they bend.

Nerves

Any of the small cells may grow a nerve cell, so a joint holds up to nine and a body up to 1,260. They form a spiking recurrent network: each has a charge that leaks, fires at a threshold, and sends weighted spikes only to the nerve cells touching it.

A muscle joint bends when the nerve cells in its patch fire. Nerves in the feet feel the ground, and every nerve cell can feel a slow pacemaker rhythm. A brain is wherever nerve cells pile up.

Evolution

Each generation adds small random changes to the genome, grows and tests every copy in parallel workers, and moves the genome toward the changes that walked farthest (an evolution strategy). When a copy beats the best walker so far on a re-test, the search jumps to it and carries on from there.

There is no designed starting creature. Random growth rules are drawn until one grows a small body of 3 to 30 joints, usually a lump that twitches. Start over draws a new one.

Take the best walker to the Python version

Copy its genome, save it as best.txt in the project folder, and turn it into a file render.py can draw:

python -c "import base64, numpy as np; np.save('best.npy', np.frombuffer(base64.b64decode(open('best.txt').read()), np.float32))"
python render.py best.npy --out best.gif
